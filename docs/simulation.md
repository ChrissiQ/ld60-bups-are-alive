# The simulation

How the simulation works: read before changing `gpu/`, `scripts/sim/`, `tests/sim/`, `tools/gpu/` or the views. The overview and the rules that hold for every kernel change (regenerating the shaders, layouts, workgroups, the main RenderingDevice) are in [AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md) under "The simulation".

- **Threads and the seam:** `SimService` (autoload, `scripts/sim/sim_service.gd`) makes the backend once (`GpuSimBackend` when `available()`, else the stub). The main thread talks to it only through `WorldClient` (`scripts/sim/world_client.gd`, reached by `WorldClient.shared()`): commands (`add_dish`, `remove_dish`, `freeze`, `thaw`, `pipette`, `resize`, `set_params`, `edit_world`, `set_time`, `set_view`, `ask`, `inspect`, `save_all`...) go in a queue, answers come back as a `SimReply`, and what the world looks like is the newest `Snapshot` (`latest()`, `snapshot_changed`). `WorldClient` hands out the lowest free client row for a dish. `SimBackend` is the seam; `tests/fakes` holds the test double. The sim thread (`SimThread`) owns a local `RenderingDevice`, a `SimCore` and a `DishWorld` per dish, and loops: drain the commands, ask the `Governor` for steps, run an iteration (record, submit, sync), copy out the dish state, stats and family events, hand them to `ResultWorker`, publish the main dish's pictures. `ResultWorker` (a second thread) applies the family events to `LineageStore`, decodes the stats and builds the `Snapshot` it swaps in under a mutex. An iteration the memory budget refuses puts the dishes' worlds back and is retried with fewer steps.
- **The pool:** `SimCore` (`scripts/sim/sim_core.gd`) is synchronous and threadless. A dish takes a row of the `DISHP`/`DSTATE` tables (`GpuLayout.D_MAX`, 1024), a 256-aligned segment of record slots, a 256-aligned block of field cells and its cloud-list squares. `SimArena` holds the buffers by unit class and grows a class by 1.5 when a batch would pass 90% of a buffer, but never past the most that class can need (record slots: the dishes' caps over the fill share; the event ring: the births bound); it shrinks a class (the event ring too, at the start of the next iteration, since an iteration's events are read after it) that used under a quarter of its slots for a minute, never below room for the most it was asked for in that time (`asked`: room made ahead for a step's worst case is not shrunk and grown back); the room an iteration is given is the bound `SimCore.births_series` gives, from each dish's count and the sum of its bups' masses (word 12 of a STATS sample, `ST_MASS`; unknown after an edit, then a bup is taken to divide every step): a bup divides at most once a step and only with mass of at least `division_mass_min` (2), and mass grows at most `growth_factor_max` a step (1.0147 at the longest step), so the births of a step are at most min(its bups, its mass / 2), which is about 240,000 bups after 14 steps from 5,000 of mass 7 where doubling gives 82 million (`tests/births_bound_test.gd` computes it from the constants and checks the kernel text it rests on; a change to the growth or division rule must re-derive it); the pictures' draw buffers and the textures made for the main device are counted in the memory budget (`SimArena.TEXTURES_SHARED` or `TEXTURES_HANDED`, by the path in use: the shared textures on the simulation's device, or on the byte path the read-back buffer and the main device's textures and staging buffers), and a refused growth publishes no picture and shows as the stalled-memory banner (the timing's `refused_iteration`); the cloud lists' entries (`ENT`, `ENTD`, 36 B an entry) are a class of their own, starting at 12 a cloud slot and grown from the entries a cloud was measured to take (the room kernel leaves the peak and the density in `WD_ENTRIES_PEAK` and `WD_ENTRY_DENSITY`, taken from the entries every square would hold (`WD_ENTRIES_FULL`, summed by the count whether a square is listed or not), so the room does not depend on which squares are listed; `SimArena.ensure_entries`, with `SimCore.ENTRY_MARGIN` 1.25 of room), since a cloud that reaches several squares takes 25-56 entries, the kernels cut a dish's lists at the real capacity (`WD_ENTRY_CAPACITY`) and at `ENTRIES_PER_CLOUD_MOST` (256) a cloud of its quota, and a cut still raises the overflow flag; a cloud with no reach (its peak is under its cut, so no bup can sense it or take from it) is in no list. **The lists cover the squares near bups only, when that pays:** `pool_bin_mark` marks each bup's own square of a dish, `pool_bin_wide_x` and `pool_bin_wide_y` widen the marks by the squares a bup can swim in a step (`SimCore._list_margin_um`: `0.25 v delta + 4 v sqrt(3 delta)` at the fastest speed a gene can reach), and the count and the scatter skip an unmarked square (a cut square still reads as empty and the overflow flag still rises). A read of an unlisted square (`bin_list` false: a bup made or moved past the margin, or a probe far from every bup) takes its clouds from the cloud slots in index order with the entry's test and values (`slot_entry`), so what it sums is what the list would have given, byte for byte. The marks pay only while a dish pool has few bups to a cloud and few squares to a cloud slot (`SimCore._marks_pay`: record slots under 2 a cloud slot, squares at most 100 a cloud slot, from the last read-back); otherwise the step lists every square (`pool_bin_clear` writes the mark of every square, the three marking passes and `pool_bin_misses` do not run, and the readers `pool_swim`, `pool_demand`, `pool_swim_demand`, `pool_apply`, `pool_apply_count` are run as their `_all` clones, made in `GpuKernels.all()` with `LISTED_ALL` defined, which have no code for an unlisted square): the marking passes cost a pass over every square, and the reader's fallback code costs it a few percent where a bup stands on a listed square always, which at 1.7M bups or at 100 dishes of 10,000 is more than the lists save (measured on Apple Silicon, Metal: 3.2 against 2.0 ms a step at 22,000 bups with the marks, 40 ms either way at 1.7M bups). `list_margin_override` -2 forces the unmarked mode. `pool_bin_misses` counts the bups in that case in `WD_FALLBACKS` (zero in the game's runs; a count since the world was made). The count is made by a kernel of its own and not by an `atomicAdd` in the reader: on Metal a never-taken `atomicAdd` on `WORLD` in the reader changed the readers' results. `SimCore.list_margin_override` is for checks only (`tests/sim/near_lists_test.gd` compares dishes run with every square listed, only the bups' own squares, and the default). The nutrient picture, which shows every cloud, reads no list: `pool_view_faint` adds every cloud that has a reach, with the mass it had when the lists were built, in fixed point, to the grid's concentration (`tests/sim/faint_view_test.gd`), and the probes add the same back for an unlisted square; `PoolLimits` has the starting sizes and the memory budget: `MachineMemory` (`scripts/sim/machine_memory.gd`) is the one meaning of the machine's physical and free memory on every platform (the engine's own figures differ by system), the card's own figures come from the native `GpuMemory.query` (`native/src/gpu_memory.cpp`: DXGI on Windows, Vulkan's device-local heaps and `VK_EXT_memory_budget` on Linux, not known on macOS, where the graphics memory is the system's; the driver handles it needs are read on the main thread by `MachineMemory.card_handles()` and passed to every later read, so the sim thread can read the card too; Godot's Vulkan driver may not enable `VK_EXT_memory_budget`, in which case the budget and usage read as 0 and count as not given), `PoolLimits.budget_for` gives the ceiling from it (shared graphics memory: 0.4 of the physical memory; a card with its own: `CARD_SHARE` 0.75 of the card's OS budget, else of its dedicated memory, never over 0.4 of the physical memory, 2 GiB when the card's size could not be read; always 1 to 8 GiB), `MemoryWatch` (`scripts/sim/memory_watch.gd`) sets `memory_budget_bytes` to a working budget every 5 s and before a growth (what the pool holds plus what it may still take: half the free system memory, or on a card that reports a live budget and usage `CARD_SHARE` of the card's unused budget, between the floor and the ceiling; it falls at once and rises with hysteresis; the arena shrinks sooner while the machine is short), `PoolLimits.classify` says whether the machine has enough, is low or is below the minimum, `PoolLimits.new()` keeps a fixed 6 GiB for tests, and `tools/gpu/mem_check.gd` is a probe of all this for any machine. The player's side of it: `MemoryMode` (`scripts/memory_mode.gd`) says whether the game is in low-memory mode (the machine is low or below the minimum; nothing the player sets changes it; with no rendering device or the stub backend there is no pool to protect, so the mode is off) and gives the limits that mode puts on a dish (`PoolLimits.LOW_BUPS` 500,000 bups, `LOW_DISHES` 10 running dishes, `LOW_GRID` 200 cells across; a loaded or thawed dish over one runs as it is and cannot be raised); a notice after the start says when the machine is below the minimum or the mode is on (`MemoryNoticeDialog` in `scenes/main.tscn`, once a run for each text, after the tutorial); a refused growth shows `banner.memory_budget`; the encyclopedia's memory entry explains it, and its numbers are the code's; `PoolLimits.warning_state` is the 90% warning the game shows as a banner. `DishWorld` (`scripts/sim/dish_world.gd`) is a dish's plankton, bloom, sources and emitters on the host: its events (clouds, deposits, husks) are stamped with the step they belong to, and `checkpoint`/`restore` undo a refused window.
- **Time:** every dish shares one world clock: `Main._advance_world` sends `WorldClient.set_time(sim_speed, paused, real_dt)`. The `Governor` (`scripts/sim/governor.gd`) turns it into steps: a step is `min(MAX_STEP, speed / STEPS_PER_SECOND)` (20 s, 120 a second), an iteration takes as many owed steps as fit `T_ITER_MS` at the measured cost of a step and `MAX_STEPS` (`GpuLayout.MAX_STEPS_PER_BATCH`) (`T_ITER_MS` bounds the steps' time alone, not the iteration's: the iteration's fixed cost, the GPU wait, read-backs and tail, comes on top, so a pause or an edit waits at most that fixed cost plus `T_ITER_MS`, plus the answer and publish; not tuned). The cost of a step is learned apart from that fixed cost: an iteration takes `fixed_ms + k x step_ms`, and `step_ms` is the slope between an iteration and the one before it at another k (until a slope is known, an iteration's time over its steps, which charges the fixed cost to the steps and holds k at 1 whenever `T_ITER_MS` over that is 1). When no iteration of another k has come for `PROBE_EVERY` iterations the next one is a probe: half the usual k, or, at k = 1 or before a slope is known, up to what twice `T_ITER_MS` allows at the pessimistic cost and at least one step more (an iteration of at most the fixed cost plus twice `T_ITER_MS`, or plus two steps where one step takes longer than `T_ITER_MS`), never within the per-frame split. A cost that rises between pairs (a dish filling) is taken at once: an iteration whose steps took more than `GROWTH_TOLERANCE` times what the known cost predicts sets the cost to what they took, restarts the pair, and leaves the fixed cost as it was, so the next iteration is shorter (a filling dish's iterations stay within about the fixed cost plus twice `T_ITER_MS`). Owed time is capped, and a duty cycle backs off when frames run long. Below `MAX_STEP` a display frame owes one frame's worth of steps (`frame_steps`) and every iteration publishes one picture: after a late iteration the catch-up runs a frame's worth at a time, each with its own publish, while the sim keeps up on its slow mean (`keeping_up`) and at most `SPLIT_OWED_MOST` frames' worth are owed; past that the owed steps merge into one iteration. With nothing owed the sim thread waits for the next command; at one step a frame, with iterations that fill at least `SPIN_SHARE` (half) of the display's frame, it spins in place of that block (`SimThread._wait_for_tick`, for at most two frames after the last `set_time`), because a thread that slept runs the next iteration's GPU wait slower (a one-step sync of about 4.3 ms against 3.0 at 100,000 bups) and the picture then misses its frame; the spinning replaces sleep-and-wake time, so the process uses about half a core either way (0.51 spinning against 0.47-0.53 sleeping). If the machine cannot keep up the whole world slows together (`achieved_speed`).
- **Dishes in the game:** `main.gd` keeps `dishes` (every running `Dish`, children of Main) and `dish`, the one in the main view; `add_dish(start)` joins one, `set_main_dish(d)` brings it into view (graph, watch list, layers, camera follow; a selection or lineage open on the old one is closed). `Dish.number` is the world's number and is never reused; the number shown (`_take_label`, 1 to 999, then the lowest free among running and frozen dishes) is a label that a thaw keeps. `ui/dish_strip.gd` (`DishStrip`) shows each running dish's tile of the thumbnails' atlas (`ThumbAtlas`, `DishThumb`, written once a second by the simulation) and is hidden with one dish. The dishes not in view keep their history from the snapshots they are handed: each holds at most `Dish.BACKLOG_MAX` samples waiting to be recorded (thinned between the oldest and the newest when more arrive, the gaps filled when they are taken, so its graph keeps its time span), and `Main._record_parked_history` records them inside a time allowance each frame. A dish that dies out pauses the game on it (`_on_dish_extinct`); resuming deletes it (`_retire_extinct`) unless it is the last running one; the last running dish dying out ends the game (`_game_over`, `EndDialog`) when the freezer is empty, and otherwise leaves it empty until a dish is unfrozen. Goal mode stays single (the pipette is locked there). `tests/dishes_test.gd`, `tools/gpu/dishes_check.gd`, `tools/gpu/dishes_perf.gd`.
- **Pipette:** `Dish.pipette(world_pos, radius)` asks the simulation for a retarget (a zero-step iteration that moves the bups in the circle to a new row, same ids; `SimCore.pipette`, kernels `pool_pip_*`; the new dish's grid is made ahead on the worker pool), the waiting pipette keeps its source dish's number and is refused if that dish has left its row by the time the grid is ready (a freed row is given out again at once; `tests/sim/pipette_row_reuse_test.gd`), and `Main._finish_pipette` adopts the new `Dish` (`adopt`) and then asks Keep, Freeze or Trash about the old one (`_ask_about_old_dish`). The family tree stays in `LineageStore` under the same ids. The watch list is one for all dishes (`WatchList.dishes`), what the layer buttons show is one choice for every dish, each dish keeps the zoom and position the camera had when it was last looked at (`Dish.view_zoom`), and with the timer on (`tool_options.pipette_timer_changed`) the game pauses every so many simulated hours until a pipette moves bups (`Toolbar.resume_locked`).
- **Husks:** `Dish.plankton_husks` and `Dish.death_husks` (`StartConditions`, advanced options) make a dying plankton or bup leave a husk instead of a cloud (`pool_husk_*` kernels, a husk buffer in the pool, `Biology.HUSK_SECONDS`). Husks have a layer of their own (`Layer.HUSKS`, default key M) and a mode of the select tool, both there only while the dish in view can have husks (`Dish.has_husks`; `LayersPanel.sync_from`, `ToolOptions.set_husks_available`). Every husk is drawn; what can be pointed at is what is drawn. The select tool's Husks mode (and Inspect all) finds one by its place (`SimCore.husk_near`, `Dish.husk_at`, `husk_details`; a husk has no id), with a hover card, a pinned panel and a ring. **The husk list is read once a hundred milliseconds at most, while the layer is shown:** `pool_husk_view` packs every live husk as a 2D MultiMesh transform (8 floats) into the husk cull's scratch buffer and counts them, and the host reads the count and the list back (`SimCore.husk_view_request`, `husk_view_take`). An iteration that steps starts the read without waiting for it and hands the list over in the next publish, so the view lags the dish by one iteration and the read does not stall the step; a quiet pass reads it with a wait (`SimCore.husk_view`). The read costs about 3 ms for 200,000 husks (measured on Apple Silicon, Metal) and is bound by the bytes moved, not by the kernel.
- **Views:** the simulation publishes the bups' payload, haze, steer and the thumbnails (`SimCore.publish`) and the main device takes them (`ViewRenderer`, `scripts/sim/view_renderer.gd`), runs the `view_*` kernels, draws the bups into a MultiMesh and answers picks. How it takes them depends on the driver (`ViewRenderer.cpu_handover()`): on Metal and Direct3D 12 every RenderingDevice shares the system's device, so the simulation shares textures and the main device imports them; on Vulkan each local RenderingDevice is a VkDevice of its own and a native handle means nothing on the main device, so the simulation reads back after its sync only the bytes in use (`SimCore._read_handed`: the payload of the bups, the pictures' pixels, the atlas's rows of tiles in use; each part is requested with `buffer_get_data_async` and one submit and sync completes them, with no intermediate host copy) and the main device puts them into textures it owns (`hand_over_picture`; the payload goes through one buffer and `pool_copy_payload_only`), with no `take_bound` bookkeeping, as nothing is shared. The ids do not cross: the simulation keeps the ids of its last `SimCore.ID_RING` publishes on its own device, a pick or hover answer carries the bup's index and the publish it was asked on, and `DishDrawing` asks `WorldClient.ids_at` (`SimCore.ids_at`) for the id of the one bup it needs, so an id arrives a few frames after the pointer rests on a bup. The payload is not packed: x and y are read as full floats by the picks, the density and the instances. `ViewRenderer.force_cpu_handover` or the user argument `--cpu-handover` takes the byte path on any driver, so it can be tested on Metal. The display shows the oldest publish it has not shown and keeps at most `WorldClient.WAITING_MOST` (2) waiting; on the shared-texture path `SimCore.TEXTURES` (10) is the ring of shared textures of each kind, which must hold `WAITING_MOST` + 6 publishes at one publish a frame. A bup shorter on screen than `Cell.MIN_VISIBLE_LENGTH_PX` is not a capsule: those bups are one picture in which each texel takes the colour of one bup that covers it (the winner, never a mean, so a crowd keeps the bups' own colours), built by the `view_*` density kernels (`ViewRenderer.set_density`, turned on by `DishDrawing._ensure_view`) and shown by `DishDrawing.density_sprite` under the capsules, only once it has been built for the dish in view; a bup at or over that length is a capsule as before, and if the density kernels do not build every bup is a capsule (`tests/sim/winner_view_test.gd`; `tools/gpu/den_shots.gd` shoots both drawings side by side).
- **A death gives back nutrient:** a bup that starves or dies by chance returns `Biology.DEATH_RELEASE` of its body (mass at `COST_PER_MASS`) as a cloud where it died (`pool_death_clouds`, `pool_death_count`); one displaced at its dish's limit (the trim) or removed by an edit returns nothing. With `death_husks` a death leaves a husk instead, and makes no cloud.
- **Starvation (stationary phase):** `Dish.stationary_phase` (`StartConditions.stationary_phase`, an advanced option in free mode; the `DPF_STATIONARY` flag of the dish) changes what happens to a bup whose reserve is at `MAINTENANCE_FLOOR`: it stops growing, pays `Biology.STARVE_MAINTENANCE` from its reserve and then from its mass (it shrinks), and dies at a constant rate that grows with its metabolism gene (`Biology.STARVE_DEATH_RATE`, `STARVE_DEATH_GROWTH_HOURS`) or when it has shrunk to `STARVE_MIN_MASS_SHARE` of its birth mass, instead of dying when the reserve is gone. Kernel: the end of `pool_life`.
- **Conjugation:** a bup's record word 14 is its donor flag (0 or 1; the drag cost is worked out from the speed gene). A donor (`Genome.DONOR_*`, `CONJ_*`) copies a run of its genes into a non-donor it touches: `pool_conj_clear`/`pool_conj_count`/`pool_conj_scan`/`pool_conj_scatter`/`pool_conj_apply` before `pool_life` (a hashed table of donors by 20 µm cell per dish; each recipient looks at the cells as far round it as the largest donor could touch it, and changes only itself). `tests/sim/conjugation_isolation_test.gd` checks that dishes do not touch.
- **Raised ceilings:** a bup's record word 12 is its `reach` (two bits a gene, speed the lowest: how many times that gene's ceiling has been raised, `Genome.REACH_*`), not its food yield, which is `exp(-harm) / sqrt(metabolism)` worked out where it is read. A raise multiplies the gene's `gene_max` in the mutation clamp (in `pool_life`), costs growth, and travels with the genes in a conjugation.
- **Acetate (the second food):** a bup that takes in more than its growth can use lets go of acetate besides (`Biology.ACETATE_*`: threshold-linear in the growth rate its intake could sustain, its own gain not reduced), and a bup with room in its reserve takes acetate up from the cell it is in, at up to 0.45 of its glucose rate and less while there is glucose. The acetate is a second field in the pool (the main field's grid and medium, no clouds, diffused with it; `DPF_ACETATE`): `pool_ac_cells` and `pool_ac_apply`, `pool_diffuse2_a`/`pool_diffuse2_b`, and the fused `pool_ac_conj_life`. Not modelled: inhibition by acetate (not significant at neutral pH), the glucose of clouds in the repression, a view of it in the game.
- **Acetate key:** `Genome.key_level(reach)` (bits 8-9 of the `reach` word: 0 none, 1 potentiated, 2 the key; the gene-ceiling code reads only the low eight): a division potentiates a level-0 line at `KEY_POTENTIATION_RATE`, a level-1 line makes the key at `KEY_RATE`; the key multiplies acetate uptake by `KEY_FACTOR` and costs `KEY_COST` of growth. The stats pass sums it as a ninth value (`key`, a graph series).
- **Divisions and generation:** a bup has `divisions` (record word 20), the divisions in its line: a division adds one to the parent, lethal daughter or not, and the daughter starts at the parent's new count, as both products of a fission are one step further on. A bup has no generation of its own. The population's `generation` is the mean `divisions` of the bups alive in the dish (the stats pass's tenth sum), as the LTEE counts generations per population; a pipette keeps the bups' counts, so the new dish carries on the count of its source.
- **Limits:** a dish's bups are held at `DP_LIMIT` with a hard cap `DP_CAP`; the trim expects the step's growth plus a margin of `CAP_SIGMAS` standard deviations, so the count stops a little short of the limit and is over it in a small share of frames (`tests/sim/cap_test.gd`).
- **The field pass is fused:** `pool_fdiff` (`gpu/kernels_field.gd`) does the feed and the diffusion of glucose and acetate in 16x16 shared-memory tiles with a halo of 3, in rounds of at most 3 iterations (an odd count of rounds, so the result always lands in `EFF` and `CONC2B`; the fixed `TILES` buffer, built by `SimCore._sync_tiles`, lists the tiles with the dishes that need the most rounds first). `pool_dep_land` copies `EFF` to `CONC` with the deposits added; `pool_clear` is out of the step (`pool_cells` zeroes the owner cells' sums). `pool_apply`, `pool_apply_count` and `pool_ac_cells` read the acetate from `CONC2B`, `pool_ac_cells` writes `CONC2`. The arithmetic and its order are the unfused pass's, so the goldens hold byte for byte; `SimCore.lone_field` runs the unfused path (`pool_feed`, `pool_diffuse_*`, `pool_dep_apply`, `pool_clear`, `pool_land_copy`) for `tools/gpu/fused_check.gd`. Field traffic is about 74 B a cell a step at 2 iterations. Per-kernel profiles of small dispatches are unreliable here: compare paths by interleaved wall-time batches.
- **The windowed checks:** `tests/sim/*_test.gd` open a window and need a GPU (`tools/run_sim_tests.sh`, a script for macOS, runs them one after another through `tools/run_windowed.sh` (which holds a lock so only one GPU run happens at a time), with a timeout each; helpers `sim_harness.gd`, `scenario.gd`, `smoke.gd`). `goldens_test.gd` compares `validate_dynamics.gd gpu <scenario> 1 6` with `tools/gpu/golden/dynamics_pooled_<scenario>_seed1_6h.txt`, byte for byte after a refactor that changes no behaviour; the goldens were made on Apple Silicon with Metal (a run is deterministic per seed on one machine, but another GPU or driver gives different numbers, so make a golden yourself there, before the change). The ad hoc checks are in `tools/gpu/` (`pool_*_check.gd`, `core_check.gd`, `view_check.gd`, `game_check.gd`...; not exported; run, on macOS, `godot --path . --rendering-driver metal --position 6000,6000 -s tools/gpu/<script>.gd -- <args>`, output to a file). Nothing else may use the GPU during timings.
- **The tests on a software GPU:** `.github/workflows/sim-tests.yml` runs `isolation_test`, `permutation_test`, `field_padding_test`, `continuation_test`, `conjugation_isolation_test`, `medium_isolation_test`, `near_lists_test`, `invariants_test`, `stats_test` and `faint_view_test` on a hosted Linux runner with no GPU, on the software Vulkan driver (lavapipe) under a virtual display (`xvfb-run`), by hand or as a job of the release. These tests need no values recorded on another machine: they compare runs made on the one device, or count (`conjugation_isolation_test` also expects a dish's donors to grow in number, `invariants_test` and `stats_test` hold sums to tolerances that were not measured on this driver, and `faint_view_test` compares the nutrient picture with a sum it works out itself). It builds the Linux debug `LineageStore` itself (at `GODOT_CPP_COMMIT`), first dispatches one trivial kernel in an empty project (`tools/ci/compute_probe.gd`, `tools/ci/probe_project/`: a failure there is the runner's, not the game's), and takes a test as passed only when its OK line is in the log. A test added to `tests/sim/` runs there only when the workflow's list names it.
- **Dispatching by the live count is not built (measured on one-dish kernels, M1 Pro).** Its kernels ran over every slot, each returning past the count, at about 0.1 ms a step per 100,000 empty slots. An indirect dispatch (`compute_list_dispatch_indirect`) **does not see a kernel's write made earlier in the same submit**, but does see a `buffer_copy` between two lists, and a copy per step needs a new compute list per step, which cost 60-85 µs a step: the net was zero at a 106,000 limit and a loss at a full dish. The pooled kernels have not been measured this way.
- The release workflow runs `--import || true`, which hides a shader that fails to import, so it then runs `tests/gpu_shaders_test.gd` and stops the release if that fails.
